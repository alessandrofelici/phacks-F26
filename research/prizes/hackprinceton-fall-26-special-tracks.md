## Inside and Out: AI for the Built World — by CoStar Group

CoStar Group built the information infrastructure of commercial real estate. CoStar is the platform the industry runs on: proprietary data on 8.5 million commercial properties — leases, sales, tenants and available space — built census-style by the largest research team in commercial real estate and used by the brokers, owners and lenders who move the market. Matterport holds the largest library of 3D digital twins of real buildings. Vexcel captures aerial imagery sharp enough to count parking spaces from the sky. No one else has the inside of the building, the view from above and the market data behind it, all for the same address.

This weekend you get to build on all of it. Bring your skills and an AI agent that uses these layers together to do something new with the built world. That might be for the people who open CoStar every morning — brokers, investors, lenders, developers, appraisers — or it might be a city planner, a delivery robot, a game, an accessibility tool or a classroom. Real estate problems are obviously close to our hearts, but they're not required; the data is. The best projects will make us say "we didn't know you could do that with our data."

Why build here:

- The real dataset for Houston, TX and Los Angeles CA, not a sample: CoStar building data, Matterport 3D scans and Vexcel aerial imagery for the same buildings, with your own API keys and a toolkit that exposes all of it via SDKs, MCP Servers and OpenAPI Services
- Direct access to the people who built these products — CoStar, Matterport and Vexcel engineers on site and in the Discord all weekend
- Spatial and visual by nature — the most fun data you'll touch this semester
- A path forward: finalists get fast-track interviews with CoStar's AI team, and winning projects get a roadmap review

What we're looking for:

- Uses at least two of the three layers and gets something out of the combination
- Agentic: reasons, plans and takes action, not a chat wrapper over a property record
- Solves a real problem for a real person — in real estate or anywhere else the built world matters
- Spatial and visual understanding, not only text

Criteria:

- 30% Use of the data — combines layers, uses them intelligently, not as decoration
- 30% Agentic depth — reasons, plans, acts; beyond chat and summaries
- 25% Usefulness — would someone want this switched on Monday? Real estate users are a natural fit, not a requirement
- 15% Demo and polish

**Prize:** 1st $1,500 · 2nd $500 · 3rd $250, cash. Finalists get fast-track interviews with CoStar's AI team. Stacks with any other prize.

## Best Practical AI Innovation — by Amazon

Build AI that solves a real problem for real people. Create AI-powered tools that go beyond demos: applications that reason, plan, take action, and adapt. Whether you're tackling healthcare, sustainability, accessibility, finance, education, logistics, or something entirely new, show us AI that someone would actually want to use every day.

Key Focus Areas:

- Agentic Behavior: Build AI that can reason, plan, and take multi-step actions
- Real-World Impact: Solve a problem that matters to real users, not just a demo
- Adaptive Intelligence: Learn from context, feedback, or user preferences
- User-Friendly Design: Make powerful AI feel simple and intuitive
- Creative Innovation: Surprise us with unexpected approaches and fresh ideas

Project Ideas (examples):

- A conversational agent that helps users compare products, read reviews, and make purchase decisions through natural dialogue
- A logistics or delivery optimization tool that uses real-time data to route, schedule, or predict delays
- An accessibility tool that uses voice, vision, or gesture to help underserved users navigate technology
- A sustainability platform that turns real-world data (satellite, sensor, public records) into actionable insights
- An agentic AI sandbox or learning environment where AI collaborates with you interactively
- A personal finance or trading tool that monitors patterns, predicts risks, and coaches better decisions
- A group coordination agent that assigns tasks, tracks progress, and nudges teammates on deadlines
- A creative AI workflow that turns raw inputs into polished content (music, video, writing, design)
- An AI layer on top of existing platforms (messaging, scheduling, browsing) that adds intelligent automation
- A multi-agent system that researches and compares options across a domain (housing, courses, jobs, travel, energy)

Evaluation Criteria:

- Creativity (25%): Is the idea original? Does it approach the problem in a surprising or clever way?
- Usefulness (25%): Does it solve a real problem someone would actually care about?
- Technical Implementation (25%): Is it well-built, reliable, and technically sound?
- User Experience (25%): Is it intuitive, polished, and something you'd want to use again?

Project must include:

- Working prototype/demo
- Clear use case and target user
- Basic documentation
- Presentation/pitch

Can use any combination of:

- Open source AI tools, libraries, and frameworks
- API services (LLM providers, search, data, vision, speech, etc.)
- Custom models or fine-tuned systems
- Agentic frameworks and tool-use integrations
- Existing platforms and infrastructure

**Prize (Amazon Gift Cards):**

- 1st Prize: $125 per person (up to 4 team members)
- 2nd Prize: $75 per person (up to 4 team members)
- 3rd Prize: $50 per person (up to 4 team members)

## Forward Deployed Challenge — by Peri

Description: Forward deployed engineers go on-site with businesses, learn how they actually operate, find where their workflows break down, and build solutions around real needs. This track asks teams to do the same. Build a project for any HackPrinceton track that solves a real problem for a real business you consulted during the hackathon: a local shop, a family business, a restaurant, a startup. The ideal entry starts with walking into the laundromat down the street, learning how they run, and building what they actually need. Student organizations and clubs also qualify.

Eligibility: Submissions must include the name of the business, the name and role of the person consulted, and one piece of supporting evidence (a screenshot of the conversation, a photo, a short video, or a direct quote).

Criteria:

- Real-world need: how clearly the project addresses a problem the business actually has.
- Usability: whether the business could realistically adopt the solution immediately.
- Execution: how complete and functional the project is end to end.

Judged by Peri, co-founded by two former Palantir forward deployed engineers.

**Prize (Forward Deployed Challenge, 1st to 3rd Place):** The top three teams will be introduced directly to the hiring teams at Peri's partner companies for a chance to interview: Cognition, Edra, Ephemeral, Sea12, Arca and Poetic. Winners also get an introduction to Peri's private talent network.

### Track 1: AI-Driven Forensics — by Arlequin AI

Investigators today face mountains of digital evidence: disk images, logs, emails, chat histories, documents. The signal that matters is often buried in it, and finding it by hand takes weeks. This weekend, we challenge you to use AI to find it in hours.

Use machine learning, deep learning or generative AI to uncover illegal or criminal patterns in forensic data. You might detect anomalies in a disk image, reconstruct a timeline of events, flag suspicious communications, link scattered pieces of evidence, or build an assistant that helps an investigator ask the right questions of a case.

Why build here:

- A real forensic challenge, close to what investigators and analysts face every day
- Optional datasets from the NIST Computer Forensic Reference Data Sets (CFReDS) to test your work on (https://cfreds.nist.gov/). You are free to use any other public or synthetic data.
- Arlequin team members available on site and on Discord to answer your questions

Project ideas (examples):

- A pipeline that triages a disk image and surfaces the files and artifacts worth a closer look
- A tool that rebuilds a timeline of user activity from scattered traces
- An agent that answers an investigator's questions about a case and cites the evidence behind each answer
- A visualization that links people, devices and events across a dataset

What we're looking for:

- AI that finds something a keyword search would miss
- Results an investigator can check and trust, with the evidence behind each finding
- A clear pipeline, from raw data to insight
- Responsible use of compute: approaches that scale well, algorithms that are efficient computationally
- Responsible use of data: public or synthetic data only, and respect for privacy

Criteria:

- Analytical depth: does the AI uncover meaningful patterns?
- Technical implementation: is the pipeline sound and reproducible?
- Usefulness: would an investigator want to use it?
- Visualization and demo

### Track 2: Social Media Analysis: Combining Content & Connectivity Patterns — by Arlequin AI

Online, what people say and who they talk to are two halves of the same story. A message can be read on its own, but its real meaning often shows up only when you also know how it spread, who amplified it and which communities it connected. Most tools look at one half. We want you to look at both.

Build your own pipeline to explore and analyze networks, combining the semantic content of messages with the connectivity patterns between actors. You might detect coordinated campaigns, map how a narrative spreads, identify the communities behind a topic, or spot accounts whose behavior doesn't match what they say.

Why build here:

- A problem at the heart of how information moves today, and of how humans and AI agents increasingly interact online
- Complete freedom in your data sources: any public social network or messaging dataset works
- Arlequin team members available on site and on Discord to answer your questions

Project ideas (examples):

- A detector of coordinated behavior that combines what accounts post with how they are connected
- A map of how a narrative spreads across communities over time
- A RAG-based assistant that retrieves the most relevant messages and actors for a given question about a network
- An interactive visualization that lets you explore topics and communities side by side

What we're looking for:

- Real use of both layers, content and connectivity, and insight gained from combining them
- Responsible use of compute: approaches that scale well, algorithms that are efficient computationally
- Clear, explorable outputs: visualizations, dashboards or reports
- Responsible use of data: public data only, and respect for privacy

Criteria:

- Combination of content and connectivity: do both layers matter in the result?
- Technical implementation: is the pipeline sound? If you use retrieval, measures such as recall@k are a plus.
- Insight: does the project reveal something non-obvious?
- Visualization and demo
